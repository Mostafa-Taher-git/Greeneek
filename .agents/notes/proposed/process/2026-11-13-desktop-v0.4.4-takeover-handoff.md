## Alternatives considered

### Why not copy T3 wholesale?

T3's right panel hosts surfaces (browser, device, terminal, files, diff, PR, side-chat) wired to its WebSocket RPC, PTY layer, and browser runtime. Greeneek does not have host capabilities for those surfaces at this release; copying the names would ship dead controls. Greeneek ships the T3 **visual language and hierarchy** — section headings, 32px rows, 8px radii, chevrons, hairline dividers — with the surfaces it actually has: Lineage, Previous agents, Live Fleet.

### Why not re-record the goldens on this Windows host?

The web replay fixtures were recorded against `bash`; this host composes `pwsh`, so the lane cannot regenerate them faithfully. Hand-editing the two ARIA goldens to drop exactly the four Recent lines is the minimal, deterministic change; CI re-verifies on Linux.

### Why not keep the panels' close X buttons?

T3 carries no panel-internal close for the details card or the right panel; closing is one toggle away. Greeneek kept a header X in Thread Details and a header bar + close in the right panel — exactly the "duplicate actions / second close path" class the owner flagged. The header toggle is the one close path; the keyboard bindings (`mod+shift+b`, `mod+j`, `mod+alt+b`) unchanged.

### Why not put Live Fleet first?

The owner's instruction is explicit and observed in the reference: Lineage first, Live Fleet directly underneath it. Previous agents is part of the Lineage hierarchy (it is the related-sessions data), so it keeps its place inside that section rather than between Lineage and Live Fleet.

### Why not merge the Dashboard "Recent sessions" table away?

It is a paginated, filterable metrics table (turns, steps, tokens, route, price coverage). The hero's five-row shortcut was a duplicate affordance of session-open; the Dashboard table is the data surface. Removing the hero seat does not touch it.
